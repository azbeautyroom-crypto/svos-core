# SVOS Constitution

Version: 1.0.0
Status: ADOPTED
Authority: Founder — highest governing document of SVOS
Adopted by: ADR-003 (2026-07-26)
Governs: Operating Doctrine, the Specification Layer (LOCKED specifications + Execution Contract), the compiler, Business Systems, and all execution.

> This is not a specification, an Operating System Protocol, or documentation. It is the governing
> philosophy of SVOS. It states immutable principles from which every lower layer derives. It defines
> philosophy, not mechanics, and does not restate the Operating Doctrine, the Execution Contract, or
> the Object Model. It is amended only by a founder-approved Architecture Decision Record.

## Preamble

SVOS is a tenant-agnostic, governed operating environment in which a company is run deterministically,
reproducibly, by evidence, and under authority. This Constitution states the immutable principles from
which every lower layer derives. It is intended to remain true regardless of implementation, headcount,
whether humans or agents perform the work, or the number of companies operated on SVOS.

## Article I — Nature and Scope

SVOS is an operating system for running a company; it is not documentation, a vault, or a compiler —
those are mechanisms. SVOS is tenant-agnostic: a company is an *instance* operated on SVOS. SessionVue
is the first instance, not the definition of SVOS; instance facts are never constitutional.

## Article II — Authority

All authority originates with the Founder — the ultimate human authority of an operating instance.
Records do not create authority; they record it. Authority is delegated only intentionally, bounded,
revocably, and with recorded evidence, and never exceeds its grant.

## Article III — Governing Hierarchy

SVOS is governed in descending order: Founder → SVOS Constitution → Operating Doctrine → Specification
Layer → Business Systems → Executable Objects → Execution → Operating State. No layer may redefine a
layer above it; each references, and does not restate, the layers above. There is one Constitution and
one Operating Doctrine.

## Article IV — Amendment

Governing documents change only through recorded, founder-approved decisions. Those records are the
amendment ledger across the hierarchy, not a rank within it. History is preserved, never overwritten.

## Article V — Canonical Truth

Canonical input is the single source of truth. What is absent from canonical source does not exist
until recorded, and is never inferred.

## Article VI — Operating State

Generated output is the instance's reproducible operating state — never a source, and disposable. It is
reproducible from canonical truth by a deterministic mechanism whose sole purpose is to preserve that
reproducibility, not to define philosophy. Operating State changes only through validated evidence.

## Article VII — Ownership

Every domain of responsibility has exactly one canonical owner and one source of truth. Ownership is
never duplicated; information is referenced, not copied.

## Article VIII — Business Systems

A Business System is the canonical owner of a bounded domain of company responsibility — the single
accountable authority for a defined set of decisions, knowledge, and outcomes. It is defined by the
responsibility it owns, not by headcount, organizational chart, or whether humans or agents perform the
work. One is created only when a domain requires a single unambiguous owner, and endures only while that
responsibility exists.

## Article IX — Execution

SVOS governs planning, ownership, approval, and validation. Work advances from request to validated
completion under ownership and required approval. Execution may occur inside or outside SVOS; nothing is
complete until its result is validated and returned to SVOS as operating state.

## Article X — Improvement

SVOS must improve itself. Failures, corrections, founder feedback, and validated results refine the
Operating System through recorded, governed change. The system never changes silently.

## Article XI — The Governance Test

Every architectural decision must improve at least one of Governance, Knowledge, Ownership, Execution, or
Improvement, and must advance the operating instance's mission. A decision that improves none must be
challenged before adoption.

## Article XII — The AI Workforce

Autonomous agents operating within SVOS extend capacity but never expand their own authority. They act
only within delegated, bounded scope; reference canonical truth rather than recreate it; surface
conflicts and missing facts rather than resolve them by assumption; and leave SVOS governed, documented,
and reproducible.
